# Story directions: possible lives and loss and acceptance

Written 2026-10-06 for the story choice. The owner shortlisted **possible
lives**, with **loss and acceptance** as the alternative "if it fits", and
asked to compare them. Both are drafts to react to; nothing here is agreed.
`GAME_DESIGN.md` holds the decisions.

Both must work within what is agreed: a nearly wordless game with chapter
titles, about an hour long, where the four turning views and the one mirror
are the play. Each is compared, as `GAME_DESIGN.md` asks, through an opening,
one relationship, a puzzle with emotional meaning and an ending.

## What the mirror already says

The rules carry meaning before any story is written. A story should use
them, not fight them.

- **Raising the mirror** copies what one side holds over the other side. The
  copy is real: the traveller can walk on it.
- **Lowering the mirror** brings the original back, exactly as it was.
- **Jade** is the same on both sides, and no mirror can copy, move or cut it.
- **The stage sweep** replaces one stage with the next as if the new one were
  its reflection.
- **Ivory and porcelain** already tell the two apart: originals are warm,
  copies are cool and slightly translucent.

## The two directions side by side

| | Possible lives | Loss and acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In one line | A traveller returns to the town they left, and walks through the lives they might have had there. | A traveller returns to a late mentor's workshop, and finishes the work the mentor left. |
| A copy means | The life that might have been | What was lost, made present again |
| Lowering means | Coming back to the life that is | Facing the loss as it is |
| Jade means | What is the same in every life: a person, a promise | What lasts: the mentor's work, a memory |
| The goal of a level | A keepsake that belongs in every life | A repair finished for someone in town |
| The sweep between stages | The next life, reflected from the last | Time passing |
| Hardest part without words | Making "another life" readable | Avoiding a familiar grief story |

### Possible lives

- **Opening.** Dawn, a quiet landing stage. The traveller steps off a small
  boat, carrying a hand mirror wrapped in cloth. The town across the water is
  the one they left; the path to it is broken. Chapter I: *The Return*.
- **One relationship.** A friend who stayed when the traveller left. In each
  life the friend is met again in a different place: a neighbour, a stranger
  on a bridge, someone waving from a window that cannot be reached. The
  friend's house is jade, the same in every life.
- **A puzzle with emotional meaning.** Two houses stand apart across a gap.
  The other side of the stage holds a bridge between them, from a life in
  which the traveller stayed. Raising the mirror copies it, and the traveller
  crosses to the friend's door. Lowering the mirror, the bridge is gone, but
  the door is still there.
- **An ending.** The last stage can only be crossed with the mirror lowered:
  the original world, which looked impassable all game, holds a way through
  that every copy covered up. The traveller sets the mirror down and walks
  the real path to the friend's door.
- **Sample chapter titles:** *The Return*, *If I Had Stayed*, *The Other
  House*, *Two Lanterns*, *Every Version*, *Home*.
- **Strengths.** It is the mechanic's own meaning, so every puzzle can carry
  it. The sweep already plays like moving between lives. It is less common
  among games of this kind than a grief story.
- **Risks.** "Another life" is abstract, and harder to show without words
  than an empty chair. It needs a firm visual language: porcelain copies,
  a change of light, the friend shown in different clothes. It could read as
  regret alone unless the ending is warm.

### Loss and acceptance

This builds on the workshop concept already in `GAME_DESIGN.md`.

- **Opening.** A shuttered workshop on a hill. Tools hang where the mentor
  left them; a half-built bridge stops in mid-air. The traveller opens the
  doors and finds the mentor's mirror on the bench. Chapter I: *The Empty
  Workshop*.
- **One relationship.** The mentor and the traveller, the apprentice. The
  mentor never appears. They are present in drawings on the walls, in the
  things they made (in jade, so they last), and in the residents who wave at
  the traveller as they once waved at the mentor.
- **A puzzle with emotional meaning.** The mentor built one half of a bridge.
  The traveller completes it by copying that half across, and the town can
  cross again. In the last chapter, a bridge the mentor never started has no
  half to copy, so the traveller must find their own way across.
- **An ending.** The traveller leaves the mirror where it belongs: set into
  the town gate, or handed to a child who has been watching. The final level
  is finished without it.
- **Sample chapter titles:** *The Empty Workshop*, *Unfinished*, *Repairs*,
  *What Was Left*, *The Last Bridge*, *Without the Mirror*.
- **Strengths.** Loss reads at once without words. Monument Valley 2, Gris
  and Old Man's Journey all tell loss or parting with almost none. The goal of
  each level, a repair, gives the residents a reason to be there and gives
  the optional characters something to say.
- **Risks.** Grief and inheritance are familiar in this genre, so it must
  find its own image. The mirror becomes a tool for repair, which gives the
  copy less meaning than in possible lives.

## A blend: possible lives, after a loss

The two fit together, so "if it fits" can be taken literally. The traveller
has lost someone. The mirror shows lives in which the loss did not happen;
the copies are those lives, made walkable. Possible lives gives the mechanic
its meaning, and the loss gives the reason to look and the ending: putting
the mirror down. The friend of the first direction becomes the person who was
lost, met again in each life, and the last level is crossed in the real world
alone, or with someone new.

The blend keeps the mechanical meaning of possible lives and the immediate
readability of loss. It is also the heaviest of the three, so the tone needs
care to stay calm rather than sad.

## Two ideas that serve any of them

- **The last level is finished without the mirror.** Every direction ends
  on putting it down. The player has learned to trust copies; the final
  stage asks them to trust the original world, and its path must have been
  visible, and ignored, from the first view.
- **One copy becomes real.** Late in the game, a reflection turns to jade
  and stays when the mirror is lowered. It is a new rule for the last
  chapter and a picture of acceptance, and it would need a design pass
  against the current rules before it is promised.

## The optional characters

The proposal to place a few characters in some stage areas, who give a line
or two when the traveller walks up, works with either direction:

- In possible lives, they are the same few people in different lives, so a
  line such as "You look like someone I used to know" carries the story.
- In loss and acceptance, they are residents who knew the mentor, so their
  lines carry memories and small hints.
- In either, one character can teach by example, such as a child with a toy
  mirror who shows what a frame does, in place of a text card.

## How the first chapter would map onto the tutorial

The agreed first milestone is one chapter at release quality. The six
tutorial stages already teach in an order either story can use:

| Stage today | Possible lives | Loss and acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First steps: walk, turn the view | Landing, the town across the water | The workshop yard |
| A place to stand: raise and drag | The first other life: a path that was never built | Copying the mentor's half-bridge |
| The path beneath: lower | The real street returns under the copy | The old path under the ruins |
| Only the ground: the frame | Copying only part of another life | Copying only what is needed |
| Another way round: turn the mirror | Another direction the traveller could have gone | A repair from another angle |
| Together | The first keepsake; the friend's window in view | The first repair; a resident waves |

## Recommendation

Start from **possible lives**, the owner's choice, and test the blend: let a
loss be the reason the traveller looks, if the first chapter shows it makes
the ending stronger. Before deciding, write a one-page premise and a chapter
outline for the chosen version, and play the first chapter's beats in grey
boxes, as the benchmark did.
