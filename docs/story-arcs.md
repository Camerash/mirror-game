# Story arcs: two drafts

DRAFT, 2026-10-30. Written for the owner to react to; nothing here is agreed.
[The vision](VISION.md) holds what is decided. Both drafts keep the
decisions so far:

- Twin sisters, always side by side, a little different: one is right-handed
  and one is left-handed, like mirror-image twins.
- She solves the puzzles hoping to save her twin, who she believes has died.
  At the end she realises that she is the one who died.
- Nearly wordless, with chapter titles. Grim on the way, light after rain at
  the end, and the last minutes light-hearted.
- A prologue, five chapters and an epilogue. The five stages of grief run
  underneath the chapters and never appear as titles.
- A title screen like Monument Valley's: the first place seen from afar; a
  tap begins.
- By minute five, one glimpse of the sister in the mirror, then she is gone.
- The finale: two parallel mirrors make an infinite pathway to what she has
  long hoped for.

Both share one wordless clue: **a pair of mittens**, one each. She carries
the left one and wants to bring her sister the right. In every reflection the
figure she follows uses the other hand. At the reveal the player sees which
sister has been the reflection all along.

The mechanics grow the same way in both:

| Part | New idea in play |
| --- | --- |
| Prologue | Walk, turn the view |
| 1 | Raise the mirror, slide it |
| 2 | Lower it; safe falls |
| 3 | The frame decides what is copied; turn the mirror |
| 4 | A second mirror |
| 5 | Two mirrors facing each other: copies of copies, an endless path |

## A. The pond

**What happened.** One winter the sisters went out on the frozen pond at the
bottom of their garden. The ice broke. Only one came back.

**The mirror is the ice.** The pond's surface is the first mirror: the line
between the world above and the world below. The game takes place on the far
side of it, and she does not know.

| Part | Place | Grief | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Title | The snowed garden from afar, the pond at its centre | | A tap begins |
| Prologue | The garden in snow | | She wakes by the pond with a hand mirror and one left mitten. In the glass, for a moment, her sister waves with her right hand. Then only snow. |
| 1. The Garden | The home garden, broken into floating pieces | Denial | She follows glimpses of her sister on the copy side, always one place ahead. Everything looks almost like home. |
| 2. The Storm | The garden in rain and wind, overgrown and wild | Anger | Places tear apart; falls; the path she builds keeps being taken away. |
| 3. If Only | The life that might have been, on the copy side: the sisters older, together | Bargaining | The mirror shows a summer that never happened. She can walk in it, but cannot stay. |
| 4. Beneath | Under the pond: dark, still water, light far above | Depression | The glimpses stop. She finds a second mirror, her sister's. |
| 5. Thaw | Both mirrors face each other | Acceptance | The endless path leads to the far end, where her sister kneels by the pond in spring, grown up and alive, laying down a right mitten. Every right-handed figure was the living sister. She is the reflection. |
| Epilogue | The garden in spring, in bloom | | Light after rain. A child, the sister's daughter, finds the hand mirror in the grass, sees something, and laughs. |

**Strengths.** The ice is the mirror, so the reveal is the mechanic. Winter
to spring is the garden look's own arc: the overgrowth is the years since,
and the bloom is what comes after. Light-hearted ending without denying the
loss.

**Risks.** A drowned child is heavy; it must be told with distance and never
shown. The thaw ending is a familiar image, so the two-mirror path must be
the moment that makes it ours.

## B. Back to the beginning

**What happened.** An accident when they were small. Only one lived. Each
chapter goes one memory deeper, as in Inception, and further back in their
life together.

**The mirror goes back in time.** Every copy is a memory of the place. The
world ages around her, but she never does.

| Part | Place | Grief | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Title | A grown woman's flat at dusk, seen from afar | | A tap begins |
| Prologue | The flat: two cups, one used | | She wakes small in a grown-up's room, holding a left mitten. In a window's reflection, a grown woman ties her hair, right-handed. Gone. |
| 1. Rooms | The sister's adult home, in pieces | Denial | Photos on the walls show one grown sister, always alone. She looks for the other. |
| 2. School | Their old school, in a storm | Anger | Corridors break apart; she builds them back, and they fall again. |
| 3. Summer | The garden of their childhood | Bargaining | The copy side shows the day before the accident. She tries to change it. |
| 4. Before | The house where they were born, dark and empty | Depression | She finds a second, smaller mirror. |
| 5. Facing | Two mirrors facing each other: every moment of their life at once | Acceptance | The endless path leads back to the start: two babies facing each other like mirrors, a lefty and a righty. In every photo she was missing because she never grew up. She lets her sister go on. |
| Epilogue | Forward again: the sister's home, morning | | Light after rain. The sister, grown, laughs at something only she can see in a window. |

**Strengths.** Going deeper and further back gives each chapter a new place
and slightly more complex models. "She never grew up" is a strong wordless
clue.

**Risks.** Layers of memory lean on Inception; "the one who never aged is
the dead one" is a familiar ghost-story twist, so the mirror must carry it.
Interiors are harder to make into floating puzzle stages than a garden.

## My recommendation

**A, the pond.** It turns the mirror itself into the place of the loss, it
fits the garden art direction and the light-after-rain feeling, and its
reveal comes out of the mechanic. B's best idea, the world ageing while she
stays small, can live inside A as a clue: the sister in the glimpses grows
older from chapter to chapter.
